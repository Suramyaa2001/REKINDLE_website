When the fire is out, the disaster is only starting.

A wildfire can leave a hillside ready to slide with the next storm. Debris flows and flash floods then cut roads, power and water to the same neighborhoods. REKINDLE studies that whole chain, from the burn to the household, and designs ways to break it.

Why this project

The January 2025 Los Angeles fires caused an estimated $53.8 billion in property damage. In 2018, debris flows in Montecito, weeks after the Thomas Fire, killed 23 people and destroyed 558 structures.

Wildfire resilience planning usually treats each of these hazards and each infrastructure system on its own. That makes it hard to see how they interact, and who ends up carrying the burden.

What we are building

Maps of where wildfires and the hazards that follow are most likely. Models of how failures spread across power, water and transportation. Measures of what disruptions cost households. And decision tools that use all of it to guide investment.

We build and test everything with communities in the Los Angeles and Montecito regions of California.

Four connected research modules

  1. Hazard risk

    Ganguly, Coleman

    A physics-informed, geospatial AI model that combines satellite data with wildfire science to map where fire, debris flows and flash floods are most likely, with honest uncertainty.

  2. Infrastructure risk

    González, Mukherjee

    Network models of how failures spread across power, water and transportation, including where data on how the systems connect is missing.

  3. Societal risk

    Clark, Serra-Llobet

    Household surveys, focus groups and simulation games that show how service disruptions affect people's daily lives, livelihoods and ability to recover.

  4. Resilience decisions

    Chatterjee, Mukherjee

    Optimization that weighs system performance against community needs to choose where to invest before a fire and how to prioritize recovery after.
