Project overview

REKINDLE helps communities in the wildland-urban interface prepare for the chain of disasters that follows a wildfire.

The problem

The January 2025 Los Angeles fires caused an estimated $53.8 billion in property damage and $8.9 billion in projected economic losses, making them one of the costliest disasters in U.S. history. Seven years earlier, debris flows in Montecito, triggered by heavy rain after the 2017 Thomas Fire, killed 23 people, injured 167 and destroyed 558 structures.

These are not isolated events. Wildfires strip vegetation and change the soil, so later storms can trigger debris flows and flash floods. Those hazards damage interdependent power, water and transportation systems, and service outages land hardest on wildland-urban interface (WUI) communities.

Current planning tends to consider each hazard and each system separately. Strategies are often set by separate organizations with misaligned goals, and judged by system-level metrics that miss what households actually lose.

Our approach

REKINDLE combines artificial intelligence, infrastructure modeling and social science to assess these risks together, from the hazard to the household. Uncertainty is carried through every step, from the first map to the final decision.

Where we work

We build and test REKINDLE with case studies in the Los Angeles and Montecito regions of California. Both have lived through major wildfires and post-fire debris flows in recent years. We work with government agencies, utility providers and community stakeholders so the tools reflect the people who will use them.

Vision

Communities in fire-prone regions can anticipate compounding wildfire risks and are supported by infrastructure systems and policies designed around their real needs.

Mission

Build the first end-to-end, uncertainty-aware framework that connects wildfire and compound-hazard prediction, interdependent infrastructure modeling and human-centered impact analysis into one decision-support system for fire-resilient, equitable communities.
