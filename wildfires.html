Cascading effects after a fire

This page does not draw live data on what happens after a fire, such as debris flows, damaged roads, water systems and power outages. These agencies publish that information themselves.

  • U.S. Geological Survey: post-fire debris-flow hazard maps
    An interactive dashboard of debris-flow hazard assessments for recently burned areas: likelihood, volume and combined hazard.
  • U.S. Forest Service: Burned Area Emergency Response
    The Forest Service program that addresses post-fire risks on Forest Service lands, such as risks to visitors, property, water quality and natural and cultural resources.
  • InciWeb
    The interagency incident information system, with a page for individual fires.
  • NIFC: National Fire News
    The national fire situation, published by the National Interagency Fire Center.
  • Oak Ridge National Laboratory: EAGLE-I power outage data, 2025
    County-level outage counts recorded at 15-minute intervals, gathered from utilities' public outage maps.

About this data

The REKINDLE team does not edit these numbers. Each figure is read from the agency's public data service when you open the page, and the map refreshes every 10 minutes while the page stays open. Agencies update and correct their records, so values can change.

NIFC's current-incident list includes fires that are fully contained until they drop off the list, which is why the map hides them unless you choose to include them. Some incidents have no reported size or containment. A fire that crosses a state line is counted in the state where it started. Incidents outside the 50 states and Washington, D.C. are listed in the table but not placed on the map.

If a chart does not load, the agency's service may be busy or offline. Try again later or use the source links under each section.
